# CodexBar Pro across platforms

Research and recommendation for [#459](https://github.com/hemsoft-dev/codexbar-ios/issues/459), checked October 17, 2026, EDT. This is a plan, not a product announcement or approval to enroll in commerce programs. No store submission, price, contract, backend or payment processor changed.

The [iOS and watchOS plan](2026-10-16-codexbar-pro-plan.md) remains the launch contract. Its free tier, no-ads decision, original-purchaser protection and Apple prices do not depend on another platform. Prices for new channels and any promise of cross-platform lifetime access need Franz's decision.

## Recommendation and sequence

Keep the account-free Apple launch. Qualify a Mac App Store build under the existing Apple app record and universal purchase first, if its sandbox can support the product. Do not open a second independent subscription catalog merely to ship another binary.

Before selling outside that Apple app family, choose one shared Pro entitlement with optional account linking for existing Apple purchasers. Recommend this over permanent per-store subscriptions because it avoids deliberately charging a person again for the same Pro tier. Buying and using the iOS product locally must remain possible without a CodexBar account. Sharing it elsewhere requires an explicit account-linking step and new privacy disclosures.

This recommendation is reversible today. Until Franz accepts the decisions below, the safe default is no additional paid channel and no website checkout. Technical packaging trials may use free or test entitlements. A research conclusion that a channel permits billing does not establish CodexBar's eligibility, publisher enrollment or expected store approval.

| Order | Work and exit condition |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | Complete the separately tracked [iOS/watchOS 2.0 release](https://github.com/hemsoft-dev/codexbar-ios/issues/470). Preserve the existing lifetime promise and offline behavior. |
| 1 | Qualify the native Mac App Store route against provider authorization, sandboxing, universal purchase and original-purchaser restore. The existing iPhone app running on Apple silicon is a different distribution case, not proof of a native Mac product. |
| 2 | Ratify the shared-entitlement boundary, account/privacy cost, original-buyer scope, processor responsibilities and launch regions. Prove the state contract with synthetic purchases before any real checkout. |
| 3 | Pilot one non-Apple channel chosen by Franz. Recommend a Windows desktop build with third-party commerce, then evaluate its Microsoft Store packaging. Do not change another repository under this issue. |
| 4 | Qualify Google Play once secure verification and subscription operations exist. Add Linux packages only after a working desktop app and updater exist. Defer other Unix targets until a concrete user need and supported build are identified. |

## Per-store purchases versus shared Pro

These are product and architecture choices. Stores remain the authority for their own payments under either model. A shared entitlement does not move an Apple subscription to Google or let one store cancel another's charges.

| Concern | Per-store Pro | Shared Pro recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Access | Each purchase unlocks only its declared app/store family. Apple universal purchase can cover supported Apple platforms within one app record. Separate records need explicit mapping. | One account can hold grants from Apple, Google, Microsoft or a website processor. Any valid grant unlocks the same Pro tier on approved clients. |
| Accounts | Apple can keep verification on device. Native Google/Microsoft billing uses the purchasing-store identity; free package installs and direct checkout have their own identity model. Direct sales need a restorable license identity; an emailed license key is still customer data. | Free use and local Apple restore stay account-free. A CodexBar account is required to share a purchase or use website checkout. Existing Apple purchasers opt in to linking. |
| Backend | No HemSoft billing backend for the Apple-only design. Google recommends server verification even for a single-store app. A per-store choice does not make every other channel serverless. | Authentication, receipt validation, purchase-to-account binding, an entitlement database, event processing, reconciliation, account deletion and support tooling are required. |
| Restore and reinstall | Restore with the purchasing store account. Installing a different store's app proves no entitlement there. | Native restore first; sign in and link verified purchases to restore elsewhere. Reinstall with no cached proof requires online verification. Signing in alone cannot invent a purchase. |
| Privacy | Store-only Apple checks preserve the current on-device model. Any other purchase service needs its own disclosure. | Account identifiers and purchase history leave the device. The current blanket no-backend description must change before release, even if users can opt out of linking. |
| Refunds and operations | Each store handles its purchase. Support must explain separate scopes and bills. | Each merchant still handles its purchase. HemSoft must propagate verified state changes across clients, monitor missed events, recover outages and answer account-linking disputes. |
| Duplicate payments | Cannot discover purchases in other stores without an identity bridge. Clear scope disclosure helps but does not prevent overlap. | Linked grants suppress redundant checkout. Unlinked identities and purchases initiated in store settings remain outside complete control; detect and explain overlap when evidence arrives. |
| Exit cost | Simple initial Apple release; later migration must reconcile customers who bought multiple subscriptions. | Ongoing service and privacy obligations. Keep merchant adapters separate so changing processors does not rewrite local provider storage. |

The present [privacy policy](../PRIVACY.md) says HemSoft has no CodexBar backend. A shared service would collect at least an account ID and purchase history; recovery by email would add contact information. Apple's [privacy disclosures](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/) require disclosure of retained off-device information, including data used only for app functionality. Reassess the entire data flow, SDKs and logs before changing any store label.

### Minimum verification and data contract

The following is a proposed implementation contract, not a claim that this service exists.

1. Give the billing account a random internal ID and a recoverable sign-in method. Select passkeys and/or verified email in the decision gate. Do not use a provider login, provider email or device identifier as the billing identity. Link a purchase only after account authentication and verified purchase ownership. Never link just because two email strings match.
2. Store the source merchant, environment, app/product ID, stable purchase-chain ID, current transaction ID, entitlement state, paid-through/grace dates, cancellation state, last verification and event deduplication keys. Keep purchase proof encrypted with restricted access. Separate sandbox and production completely.
3. Bind each purchase chain to at most one billing account. An authenticated transfer requires proof of both accounts or a documented recovery process. A conflicting claim must explain the conflict and preserve the existing owner's access, not silently move it.
4. Validate signatures, app identity, product allowlists, environment and current merchant state before granting. An Apple [`appAccountToken`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appstoreserverapi/appaccounttoken) associates an app-generated UUID with a transaction; it is not proof that a caller owns that UUID. Use [App Store Server Notifications V2](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appstoreservernotifications) and server reconciliation for linked Apple purchases. Existing transactions without a token need a verified restore-and-link flow.
5. For Google subscriptions, verify tokens on the server with `purchases.subscriptionsv2.get`; RTDN prompts reconciliation, not a grant. Grant completed purchases and acknowledge every new purchase token within three days to avoid automatic refund/revocation. This includes upgrades, downgrades and re-subscriptions; ordinary renewals do not need acknowledgment. See the [subscription lifecycle](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/lifecycle/subscriptions). For lifetime, use a non-consumable one-time product, verify with `purchases.productsv2.getproductpurchasev2`, acknowledge without consuming, and reconcile one-time RTDN/voided purchases. The same acknowledgment deadline applies. See the [one-time lifecycle](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/lifecycle/one-time). Keep verification credentials off the app.
6. For Microsoft commerce, use the authenticated collection/recurrence integration supported by the chosen package. For direct checkout, verify the processor's signed event and query authoritative payment/subscription state. A browser success redirect, client boolean or email receipt never grants Pro. [Stripe's webhook guidance](https://docs.stripe.com/webhooks) documents signatures, duplicate events and unordered delivery; it is an example of the contract, not a processor selection.
7. Reduce independent verified grants to one Pro result. Removing one refunded grant must not remove an unrelated valid lifetime grant. Reject stale events that would resurrect a revoked purchase; periodically reconcile authoritative state to recover missed events. A signed client cache contains the minimum access decision, account subject, source scope, version and expiry, not raw receipts.

Provider credentials, OAuth sessions, provider account names, balances, usage data, history, dashboard settings and widget snapshots must never enter billing requests, processor metadata or billing logs. Provider data stays in existing local secure storage. Redaction and payload-allowlist tests must cover errors as well as successful requests. Sharing Pro does not sync accounts or usage.

### State transitions and customer outcomes

The Apple-only column preserves the [existing plan](2026-10-16-codexbar-pro-plan.md#entitlement-model). The shared column is a recommendation requiring ratification and test fixtures.

| Event | Apple-only behavior | Shared behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pending, canceled or failed purchase | No new Pro grant. An existing valid grant survives. | Same. Hold a checkout attempt as pending; do not invite another payment while settlement is unknown. |
| Verified trial or paid subscription | Pro until verified expiry and any active billing grace. | Apply that source grant to the linked account. Show merchant and renewal date on every client. A trial is not a new trial on each device. |
| Renewal | StoreKit updates access. | Authenticate notification, reconcile and extend the same grant once. Replayed or late events cannot add time twice. |
| Auto-renew canceled | Keep access through the paid period. | Same on all linked clients. Cancellation is not refund or immediate expiry. Management stays with the originating merchant. |
| Payment failure | Use verified store billing grace; no grace means free when the paid period ends, subject to the offline rule. | Never invent merchant grace. Account hold, paused access or expired grace removes that source grant when verified; another valid grant still wins. |
| Refund/revocation | Remove the affected source when StoreKit reports it. | Reconcile merchant entitlement state and revoke that source across clients. A partial monetary refund alone does not prove full access revocation. Do not infer cancellation from a refund; verify both. |
| Monthly/yearly change | The same-feature products in one Apple group follow the existing crossgrade policy. | Change at the source merchant using its documented replacement rules. Publish the verified effective date; no manual copying of paid time between stores. |
| Switch to another merchant | Not a supported Apple-only operation. | Turn off renewal at the old merchant, verify it, wait for its paid access and grace to end, then offer the new purchase. No automatic early second charge or promise of cross-store proration. |
| Switch to lifetime | The existing Apple plan requires verified auto-renew off and explains overlapping paid time. | Suppress lifetime checkout while any linked subscription will renew. Recommend waiting for paid access to end across merchants; any intentional early overlap needs explicit disclosure and Franz's policy. |
| Sign out or account switch | StoreKit still reflects the local Apple purchaser. | Erase the shared account's local token/cache; recompute any independently valid local store grant. Never show another signed-in account's shared entitlement. No provider credentials are deleted as a billing side effect. |
| Reinstall/new device | Native restore with the same Apple account. | Sign in for shared restore, then obtain a fresh signed decision. A receipt already bound elsewhere triggers recovery, not a new sale. |
| Pro ends | Preserve credentials/settings/history and let the user choose the one free account. | Same observable downgrade contract on every implemented platform. No data deletion on subscription expiry. |

For shared subscription caches, recommend the existing three-day offline allowance after the latest verified paid-through or merchant-grace end. It is a CodexBar concession, not a store rule. Check online when possible; a known revocation overrides the allowance immediately. Never extend it by relaunching, replaying an old cache or rolling back the clock. First launch without proof stays free.

For shared lifetime grants, recommend a 30-day signed offline lease, refreshed online, so revocations and account changes eventually reach disconnected devices. That is different from the existing Apple-only unlimited cached lifetime access and needs explicit approval and disclosure. The original local Apple promise stays intact. No system can revoke access instantly on a device that remains offline; the maximum delay must be stated and tested.

### Account deletion and recovery

Provide deletion within every account-enabled app and on the website. Apple's [account-deletion guidance](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-review/guideline-reference/5-1-1-account-deletion) requires an in-app path, explains continuing Apple charges and permits immediate deletion even when later deletion is offered. Google's [account-deletion requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111?hl=en) also require a working web route for applicable apps.

Before confirmation, list active merchants and explain that deleting the CodexBar account does not cancel their subscriptions. Offer each merchant's management flow, and offer deletion at expiry as a convenience. Keep immediate deletion available. Reauthenticate, invalidate server sessions and shared grants, delete the account and optional data, and show completion. Connected clients discard shared tokens; offline clients reach the disclosed lease deadline. Do not delete local provider settings without a separate user action.

Franz must approve a specific retention schedule before launch. Retain only transaction records that the selected processor, accounting obligations or defensible fraud prevention actually require, with a stated purpose and expiry. Do not promise complete erasure while secretly keeping an identifiable license ledger. Merchant records remain under the merchant's own policy. Restore after deletion requires fresh merchant proof and a newly consented account binding; no automatic resurrection of deleted profile data. Conflicting retained bindings need a recovery path that does not demand a second payment.

### Preventing duplicate payment

Before checkout, restore local purchases and reconcile all linked grants. Display the active merchant and management action instead of purchase buttons. Original-purchaser and lifetime grants suppress recurring offers. A backend transaction reserves one checkout attempt per account, uses processor idempotency keys and reconciles pending attempts before creating another session.

An account can still buy through another store account, a different CodexBar identity or the merchant's subscription settings. Never promise global prevention. When those grants are linked, retain access once, show both charge sources and explain how to cancel or request a refund from the appropriate merchant. Do not silently cancel, refund, merge identities or credit paid time. Account-free Apple use cannot reveal an unlinked purchase on Windows; its purchase screen must disclose the sharing step before a new charge.

## Original iOS purchasers

The existing promise is lifetime Pro in the iOS app and its watch companion, including the accepted pre-cutover Family Sharing case. This issue makes no new entitlement commitment. A native Mac binary, a direct Mac download and a Windows product are not automatically covered by the phrase "original purchaser."

Recommend preserving the Apple promise and offering those verified original purchasers a free shared lifetime grant if Franz elects shared Pro. Do not make them subscribe again to claim it. Until that decision and implementation, other-platform access is unpromised and new paid launches remain gated.

The future claim flow starts in the original Apple app after native verification, then asks for optional billing-account linking. Verify the signed app transaction on the server against the original app identity and the cutover policy, bind one grant and return the signed shared decision. Apple's [Get App Transaction Info](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appstoreserverapi/get-app-transaction-info) provides signed app-purchase information. Its [`appTransactionID`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/apptransaction/apptransactionid) is stable for an Apple account and app and distinct for each family member; it does not prove the historical Family Sharing eligibility rule by itself.

Do not apply the iOS build-number cutoff unchanged on Mac, even under universal purchase. Apple's [`originalAppVersion`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/apptransaction/originalappversion) uses the marketing version on macOS and build number on other platforms. Native Mac qualification needs platform-aware evidence/version parsing, the correct app identity and verified product restoration before any portability promise. Keep the existing Family Sharing ambiguity and support fallback from the iOS plan; a server does not retroactively establish an unrecorded family install date. A verified original app [`revocationDate`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/apptransaction/revocationdate) removes the shared grant when observed. An absent signal is not proof that all old refunds were detected.
